# Why profit lags revenue

## The question
Between 2011 and 2014, revenue grew 57% ($179,068 to $280,924) but profit grew only 47% ($19,020 to $27,953). In 2014 alone, revenue rose 7.4% while profit fell 36.0%, and margin dropped from 16.7% to 10.0%.

## The answer in one paragraph
Profit lagged revenue mainly because of heavy discounting. Lines discounted above 20% are only 14.7% of revenue but lose $54,254 (a -41.7% margin), roughly half of total profit. The share of revenue sold at that discount level rose from 11.0% in 2013 to 16.9% in 2014. Revenue growth also leaned on low-margin products (Machines, Tables), and a handful of states (Texas, Illinois, Pennsylvania) lose money because they are discounted so deeply.

## Evidence by hypothesis

| # | Hypothesis | Verdict | Key number |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discount erosion | Confirmed | 18.6% of lines lose money, destroying $61,764. Margin is 30.0% at 0% discount, -16.5% at 21-40%, and -88.1% at 40%+ |
| 2 | Mix shift to low-margin products | Partly confirmed | In 2014 revenue rose $19,409 but the growth effect was -$1,139, so new revenue came from lower-margin products. Tables (-$6,253), Bookcases (-$1,215) and Supplies (-$959) lose money overall |
| 3 | Returns | Not tested | The dataset has no Returns sheet |
| 4 | Regional drag | Confirmed | 9 of 47 states lose $40,488 in total. Texas, Illinois and Pennsylvania have average discounts of 32-39% |
| 5 | Customer quality | Partly confirmed | Repeat customers are 78.1% of customers but 91.0% of profit, and the top 10% of customers generate 71.7% of profit. But one-time customers did NOT get deeper first-order discounts (15.6% vs 15.4%), so the "acquired with deep discounts" idea is rejected |

## Profit bridge (2013 to 2014)
Growth effect: -$1,139. Margin effect: -$14,575. Net change in profit: -$15,714.
Revenue rose, but the extra revenue was earned at lower margins, and margins fell inside existing products too. Machines (-$6,775) and Tables (-$3,802) had the biggest margin erosion.

## Recommendations
1. Cap discounts at 20% and require approval above that. Lines above 20% lose $54,254, and lines at 40%+ lose 88 cents per dollar of sales.
2. Reprice or rationalise Tables, Bookcases and Supplies, and review Machines pricing.
3. Fix pricing in Texas, Illinois, Pennsylvania, North Carolina and Tennessee, where average discounts are 29-39% and profit is negative.
4. Protect and grow the profitable customers. Win back the "At Risk" segment (24.1% of revenue but 30.4% of profit) and look after the top 10% of customers who bring 71.7% of profit.

## Expected impact
If the lines discounted above 20% had only broken even, profit would be about $164,602 instead of $110,348 (+49%), and margin would be 18.6% instead of 12.5%.

## Caveats
- Superstore is a sample dataset, so these findings demonstrate the method.
- 2013 was an unusually strong year (profit +121.6%), which makes the 2014 drop look larger.
- The dataset has no cost breakdown, so profit is taken as recorded.
- No returns data was available, so hypothesis 3 was not tested.
